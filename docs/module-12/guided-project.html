Guided Project: Statistical Transformations and Coordinate Systems

CIS-3901 · Module 12

Purpose

In Module 12 you learned that ggplot2 computes before it draws (stats), and that the frame a plot is drawn in can change what a reader sees (coordinates). In this Guided Project you use both on your own Fairness and Sports data, and you publish your findings as a Quarto report on your own website, the one you set up in Module 10.

You’ll practice turning counts into rates (and choosing the total you divide by), showing a summary with its n and its spread, deciding between zooming and filtering, and choosing the frame (sideways bars). These are the moves your Challenge Project reports will need.

What you’ll hand in

  1. Your published report (a web page on your GitHub Pages site), showing your code and its output.
  2. In Blackboard: the link to your report (step 6).
  3. AI disclosure: AI use line in your report, AI log typed in Blackboard (or the no-AI statement), and copies of all AI conversations uploaded in Blackboard (step 6).

Words you’ll use

  • Share: a part ÷ a total. It has no unit. Always say of what.
  • Rate: a share of a group that has an outcome: outcome count ÷ group size (e.g., a school’s graduation rate). This is question A.
  • Question B: of everyone with the outcome, what share is each group? Same counts, a different total.
  • sd = spread of the individuals; se = spread of the mean (or rate): how much it would move with another sample of the same size.
  • Lie Factor (Tufte, 2001, p. 57): how many times a picture exaggerates a difference.

Steps

All steps happen in your cis-3901-projects project in Posit Cloud (course space CIS-3901-Fall2026), not in a new assignment. Keep this page open while you work.

Step 0 · One-time setting (1 minute)

Tools → Project Options → General: set Restore .RData into workspace at startup to No and Save workspace to .RData on exit to Never. Then Session → Restart R. (This stops old objects from coming back and hiding errors.)

Step 1 · Bring your two data files (5 minutes)

Your verified Fairness file (fairness.csv) and your Sports file (sports.csv) are in the data/ folder of your Module 11 Guided Project, guided_project_aes_geoms.

  1. Open guided_project_aes_geoms. In the Files pane, open data/, tick only fairness.csv → More → Export → Download. Do the same for sports.csv. (One file at a time: ticking both downloads a zip.)
  2. Open cis-3901-projects. In the Files pane:
    • go to data/tidy/ → Upload → fairness.csv;
    • go to data/sports/ → Upload → sports.csv.

Names matter: exactly data/tidy/fairness.csv and data/sports/sports.csv. If your browser renamed a download (for example fairness (1).csv), rename it in the Files pane (tick it → Rename). If you upload a file to the top folder or to data/ by mistake, the script moves it to the right place and tells you.

Your data files are published with your report. You push them to your public repository in step 5, so anyone can see them, as with your report.

Privacy: private people’s names must not be in your files or in your plots (for example, runners’ names in the Boston Marathon data). Professional athletes, teams and officials are public figures and are fine.

Ideas for your columns (check them with glimpse())

Use these as starting points; check them with glimpse() in your own file. Every class dataset gets the same kind of hint. Small groups stay in your plots, with their n.

Fairness

  • Adult: group sex or race; outcome income == ">50K".
  • ACS Income: group sex or str_trunc(race, 30) (the full race labels are too long for a plot, and some groups are very small: one has n = 1, so a3_start must be below 1); outcome income > 50000.
  • Diabetes Hospital: group race or age (three bands); outcome readmit_30_days == 1.
  • Credit Card (fairness.csv = your clients file): group sex or education (one group has n = 2); outcome default_next_month == "yes".

Sports

  • NFL Draft Combine: group position; outcome Round %in% 1 (picked in round 1; Round is empty for players not drafted, and %in% counts them as FALSE); value forty.
  • WNBA game statistics: group team_home_away; outcome team_winner. The file has no score column, so use the days since the team’s previous game as the value: start with sports %>% arrange(game_date) %>% group_by(team_id, season) %>% mutate(...), with value = as.numeric(game_date - lag(game_date)) inside mutate(), then add %>% ungroup(). Home and away have the same count, so B3’s Lie Factor is 1.
  • Setka Cup table tennis: group sets played, factor(Sets_P1 + Sets_P2) (3, 4 or 5; factor() makes them groups, not a number line); outcome HomeWinner == 1; value points in game 1, P1_G1 + P2_G1.
  • Sumo: group the rank’s first letter, str_sub(Rank, 1, 1); outcome wins > losses; value weight_kg or height_cm.
  • WTA Grand Slam matches: group surface or round; outcome minutes > 120 (or a threshold you choose); value minutes. Some matches have no minutes: they show as NA in the legend of A1-style plots.
  • MMA fight decisions: group factor(rounds) (3, 5 or 12); outcome agreement == "Agree" (all three judges agree); value abs(judge1_margin). (Not result_type: Unanimous almost always means Agree, so its rates are close to 0% or 100%.)
  • MLB pitches: group player_name or pitch_type (includes NA and types with n = 2 or 5: they stay, with their n); outcome result == "S" (a strike); value release_speed.
